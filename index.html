No Statewide Election Scheduled

South Carolina holds its general election on the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November of each even-numbered year, and its statewide primary on the second Tuesday in June of those same years.

Odd years still carry municipal elections, school board races, and special elections called to fill a vacancy. This page cannot show you those. They are scheduled ad hoc rather than by statute, and this page has no data connection — it only does arithmetic on dates baked into the file, so a special election called next month would leave no trace here. The state's list is the authority, and it is refreshed every two weeks.

The Full Sequence

Getting Registered & Voting Early Election Day Window Counting & Certification

Early Voting: Monday through Saturday for the two weeks before the election, 8:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., closed the intervening Sunday. Any early voting center in your county, photo ID required, same machines as Election Day.

Absentee Ballots: must be received by your county board by 7:00 p.m. on Election Day — a postmark is not enough. Military and overseas ballots are accepted for two additional days.

On Election Day: you must vote at your own assigned precinct, unlike early voting.

Special Elections: see the dropdown above the sequence. Those are hand-entered and stamped with the date they were written in, because special elections are called ad hoc to fill a vacancy — nothing about them follows from statute or the clock. This page has no data connection and never fetches anything, so one called after that stamp will not appear. The state's own list is the authority, and it is refreshed every two weeks. 2026 dates are transcribed from the South Carolina Election Commission 2026 Election Calendar and scvotes.gov, read 16 Aug 2026. Later years are computed from statute — S.C. Code §7-13-10 (election day), §7-13-25 (early voting period and hours), and §7-13-40 (primary date), with the 30-day registration cutoff from scvotes.gov — and are projections, not certified dates. The Election Commission publishes the binding calendar for each year separately, and the legislature can change any of these rules. Reconfirm at scvotes.gov before relying on a date near its cutoff.

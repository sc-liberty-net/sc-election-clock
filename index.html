No Statewide Election Scheduled

South Carolina holds its general election on the first Tuesday after the first Monday in November of each even-numbered year, and its statewide primary on the second Tuesday in June of those same years.

Odd years still carry municipal elections, school board races, and special elections called to fill a vacancy. Those are in the panel above — this page pulls the state's own calendar automatically, so a special election called next month will appear here on its own.

The Full Timeline

Getting Registered & Voting Early Election Day Window Counting & Certification

Early Voting: Monday through Saturday for the two weeks before the election, 8:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., closed the intervening Sunday. Any early voting center in your county, photo ID required, same machines as Election Day.

Absentee Ballots: must be received by your county board by 7:00 p.m. on Election Day — a postmark is not enough. Military and overseas ballots are accepted for two additional days.

On Election Day: you must vote at your own assigned precinct, unlike early voting.

Special & Local Elections: see the panel above the timeline. That list is pulled straight from the South Carolina Election Commission's published calendar on a schedule, so special elections called to fill a vacancy show up without anyone editing this page.

Special and local elections come from the Election Commission's own upcoming elections calendar, re-checked automatically every six hours. 2026 statewide dates are transcribed from the 2026 Election Calendar. Later years are computed from statute — S.C. Code §7-13-10 (election day), §7-13-25 (early voting period and hours), and §7-13-40 (primary date), with the 30-day registration cutoff from scvotes.gov — and are projections, not certified dates. The Election Commission publishes the binding calendar for each year separately, and the legislature can change any of these rules. Reconfirm at scvotes.gov before relying on a date near its cutoff.
